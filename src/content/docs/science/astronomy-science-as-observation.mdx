---
title: Astronomy — Science as Observation
description: In astronomy, you cannot touch it. You can only observe.
---

If you consider the stars in the night sky, you might instinctively think of them as white dots, blinking in a dark background.
If you live in a city or in an area where there is a lot of artificial light, it is difficult to see any but the brightest stars, even on a clear night when the moon is not dominating the sky.

But if you are in an area free from city lights and you allow your eyes time to adjust to the darkness, after a few minutes of gazing at the sky and with some concentration you will begin to see that the stars appear in a variety of colors, from shades of red, orange, yellow, and blue.
Their apparent sizes vary, as well as their brighness.
There is nothing standard at all about the stars.

Remain out in the field or wherever you are for a longer period of time and you will notice change.
The position of the stars relative to the horizon or objects like trees or buildings changes as time goes by.
The sky appears to rotate, the stars tracing out arcs of motion as the night progresses.
Some stars dip below the haze of the horizon while new stars rise above and make their debut.
If you were to come out over the course of several clear nights, you might notice a few very bright objects shift positions relative to the less bright stars which appear more fixed in their positions night after night.
With time, you might come to realize these objects are not stars, but the planets of the solar system, reflecting the light of the sun just as the moon does rather than shining on their own.

There are puzzles and mysteries to discover in the night sky.
Colors.
Motions.
Different sorts of objects.

This fascination with the night sky is the ancient human attraction to the study of the science of astronomy.
With astronomy the desire to touch and feel can at times seem frustrating.
The objects are too far away, can't be touched, can only be seen and observed.
The eye is king and light is the muse.

With astronomy, human imagination and creativity soars.
Rotate your perspective with your mind imagine, if you can, your body aligned with the solar system's axis.
Try to think of yourself aligned with the earth, the sun, and the planets and see the orbits from a different perspective than from your feet planted on Earth.
If you can, your mind can now zoom about the solar system and see the stars from a different perspective.
You still can't touch anything, but the light comes to you in a different way.

If the moon is up, have a look at it.
It's lit by sunlight, light reflected from the sun which bounces off the surface of the moon which then reaches your eye.
Put your hand out.
It's bathed in moonlight.
Photons that left the surface of the sun bounced off the surface of the moon and then struck your hand.
You are touching light that only just now visited the moon.

Perhaps you can touch in this science of astronomy, after all.
